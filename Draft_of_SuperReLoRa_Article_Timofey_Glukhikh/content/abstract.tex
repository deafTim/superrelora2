\begin{abstract}
Large language models are becoming increasingly larger, with the number of parameters growing correspondingly.
Training or adapting them with full-rank updates is expensive, while a single low-rank adapter has limited expressive power.
We propose SuperReLoRa, a multi-cycle extension of ReLoRA that periodically merges LoRA adapters into the base weights and reinitializes them.
Unlike ReLoRA, which relies on random reinitialization, SuperReLoRa enforces column-space orthogonality of successive adapters at each reinit, so that new cycles start in input subspaces disjoint from previous ones and the accumulated update can increase in rank.
Because merges often occur before a cycle has fully converged, we further introduce transition momentum: a partial merge combined with a convex blend of old and newly orthogonalized adapters (and a soft carry-over of Adam moments) that smooths the handover between cycles.
We compare SuperReLoRa against LoRA and ReLoRA under a matched training budget.
\end{abstract}
