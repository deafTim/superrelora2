\paragraph{Parameter-efficient fine-tuning.}
Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) reduces the cost of adapting large pretrained models by updating only a small subset of parameters.
Early adapter methods introduced compact trainable modules for transfer learning in vision~\cite{rebuffi2017,rebuffi2018} and NLP~\cite{houlsby2019,pfeiffer2021}.
Alternative PEFT families include bias-only tuning~\cite{benzaken2022}, prompt- and prefix-tuning~\cite{lester2021,li2021}, and other lightweight reparameterizations~\cite{he2022}.
Our work follows the low-rank adapter line rather than sequential modules or prompt-based approaches.

\paragraph{Low-rank adapters.}
LoRA~\cite{hu2021lora} freezes pretrained weights $W$ and learns a low-rank update $\delta W = s W_A W_B$.
Subsequent variants improve allocation or efficiency of this factorization: AdaLoRA~\cite{zhang2023} adapts rank via SVD-based parameterization, DoRA~\cite{liu2024dora} decomposes updates into magnitude and direction, VeRA~\cite{kopiczko2024vera} shares frozen random projections across layers, and QLoRA~\cite{dettmers2023} combines LoRA with quantized base weights.
Practical implementations further reduce memory and compute~\cite{cherniuk2023,dettmers2023}.
These methods typically train a \emph{single} low-rank adapter of fixed rank $r$, and therefore do not explicitly accumulate a higher-rank total update over the course of training.

\paragraph{High-rank training through low-rank updates.}
ReLoRA~\cite{lialin2023stack} observes that repeated merge-and-reinitialize of LoRA adapters can approximate a higher-rank update
$\Delta W = \sum_{i} s W_A^{i} W_B^{i}$
while keeping the optimizer state small.
After each merge into $W$, ReLoRA hard-resets the adapter matrices and partially resets Adam moments~\cite{kingma2017adam}, because retained moments otherwise continue to optimize the previous subspace; it also uses a jagged learning-rate schedule with warm restarts.
Random reinitialization encourages, but does not guarantee, that successive adapters explore new directions: nothing prevents a new cycle from recovering the same column space as an earlier one.

\paragraph{Our positioning.}
Prior PEFT methods either keep a fixed low-rank adapter~\cite{hu2021lora,zhang2023,liu2024dora,kopiczko2024vera,dettmers2023} or periodically merge adapters and hard-reset them to accumulate higher-rank updates~\cite{lialin2023stack}.
SuperReLoRa follows the multi-cycle merge-and-reinit template of ReLoRA, but additionally enforces \emph{column-space orthogonality} of the new $W_A$ against an accumulated orthonormal basis of past adapters at each reinit.
The goal is that successive LoRA cycles start in disjoint input subspaces, so that the summed update $\Delta W$ can increase in effective rank with the number of cycles.
