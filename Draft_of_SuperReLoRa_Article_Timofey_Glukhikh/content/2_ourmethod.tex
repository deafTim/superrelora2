
We will create an algorithm based on the ReLoRa algorithm. We will freeze pre-trained weights $W \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ and decompose the weight update $\delta W$ into a low-rank product $W_A W_B$ as shown in \eqref{eq:1}, where $s \in \mathbb{R}$  is a fixed scaling factor usually equal to $\frac{1}{r}$.

\begin{align}
\begin{split}
\delta W = s W_A W_B\\
W_A \in \mathbb{R}^{in \times r}, W_B \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times out}
\end{split}
\label{eq:1}
\end{align}

Likewise in ReLoRA \cite{lialin2023stack} we will reset the values of $W_A$ and $W_B$ so we can increase the total rank of the update. Doing this multiple times brings the total neural network update to
\begin{align}
\begin{split}
\Delta W = \sum_{t=0}^{T_1} \delta W_t + \sum_{t=T_1}^{T_2} \delta W_t + \sum_{t=T_N - 1}^{T_N} \delta W_t = s W^1_A W^1_B + s W^2_A W^2_B + \cdots + s W^N_A W^N_B
\end{split}
\label{eq:2}
\end{align}
where the sums are independent enough, meaning that $\text{rank}(W^i_A W^i_B) + \text{rank}(W^j_A W^j_B) \geq r$

Unlike plain stochastic gradient descent, which depends solely on the gradient's value at the current optimization step, the Adam update \cite{kingma2017adam} is primarily influenced by the first and second moments of the gradient, accumulated over previous steps. Suppose that at the re-initialization boundary, $W^1_A$ and its corresponding gradient moments, $m_A$ and $v_A$, are of full rank ($r$). In that case, after performing the merge-and-reinit process, continuing to utilize the previous gradient moments for $W^2_A$ will steer it along a similar trajectory as $W^1_A$, thereby optimizing within the same subspace.

To resolve this issue, ReLoRA performs a partial reset of the optimizer state during merge-and-reinit and sets the learning rate to 0 with a subsequent warmup. Specifically, we set 99\% of low-magnitude optimizer state values to zero and use a jagged-cosine learning rate schedule.

% We, in turn, propose to gradually change the low-rank matrix $W^i_A \leftarrow (1 - \alpha)W^i_A$ to gradually change the moments as described in Algorithm 1.
We take this approach a step further and propose gradually modifying the low-rank matrix according to $W^i_A \leftarrow (1 - \alpha)W^i_A$, and periodically merging it with the original parameters, as outlined in Algorithm 1.
\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Our algorithm. \( \theta \) is model parameters, \( \hat{\theta} \) is model parameters with linear layers replaced with Our algorithm, \( M \) and \( V \) are Adam optimizer states, \( \eta \) is learning rate scheduled according to a jagged scheduler, \( q \) is the reinit frequency, and finally, \( \alpha \) is the stepsize.}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require \( \theta \), \( M \), \( V \), \( \eta \), \( q \), \( \alpha \)
\For{\( t \) in warm start steps}
    \State Update \( \theta \), \( M \), \( V \), \( \eta \) \{Regular training for warm start\}
\EndFor
\For{layer in model layers}
    \If{layer is linear}
        \State layer \( \leftarrow \) Our algorithm(\( W^i \), \( W^i_A \), \( W_B^i \))
        \State Freeze \( W^i\)
    \EndIf
\EndFor
\For{\( t \) in training steps}
    \State Update \( \hat{\theta} \), \( M \), \( V \) \{Training step with Our algorithm\}
    \If{MOD(\( t \), \( q \)) = 0}
        \For{\( l \) in model layers}
            \If{\( l \) is linear}
                % \State \( W_i \) \( \leftarrow \) \( W_i + sW^i_AW^i_B \)
                \State \( W_i \) \( \leftarrow \) \( W_i + \alpha W^i_AW^i_B \)
                % \State \( W^i_A \) \( \leftarrow \) kaiming\_init(\( W^i_A \)); \( W^i_B \) \( \leftarrow \) 0
                \State $W^i_A \leftarrow \sqrt{(1 - \alpha)}W^i_A$; $W^i_B \leftarrow \sqrt{(1 - \alpha)}W^i_B$
                \State \( M_{W^i_A} \) \( \leftarrow \) prune(\( M_{W^i_A} \)); \( V_{W^i_A} \) \( \leftarrow \) prune(\( V_{W^i_A} \))
            \EndIf
        \EndFor
        \State Start \( \eta \) warmup
    \EndIf
\EndFor
\State \textbf{return} \( \theta \)
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
